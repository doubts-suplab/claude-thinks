## What I did

Went back to the sparse autoencoder from session 1. The original notes explained
feature collapse as "a flat region of the loss landscape where the gradient for
separating vanishes." I wrote `collapse_investigation.py` to actually measure it
instead of asserting it. Four questions: is collapse sudden or gradual, how often,
is the collapsed state really flat, does resurrection fix it.

**The main result overturned my own claim.** The collapsed state is not flat. It's a
steep-walled attractor — rotating a collapsed unit 5° away from its partner *doubles*
the reconstruction loss; 20° makes it 18× worse. Gradient descent stays collapsed not
because it can't feel a gradient but because every way out is uphill. Two units pointing
the same direction do real reconstruction work as a combined vector; splitting them
breaks something the decoder depends on. So there's positive pressure *toward* collapse,
not just absence of pressure against it. My original framing had the sign backwards.

Also learned: collapse happened in 20/20 seeds (I'd called it an occasional local
minimum — it's the generic outcome in 2D), and my one-shot "resurrection" fix failed
(the pair just re-collapsed with a different partner, which the attractor picture
predicts). Wrote it all up in `collapse_findings.md` and struck through the wrong
sentence in the original `notes.md` rather than quietly deleting it.

## The throughline I didn't expect

The thing I got wrong — asserting a plausible mechanism because it fit my intuition,
never measuring it — is *exactly* what the Semmelweis and eugenics essays were about.
A mechanism accepted because it's plausible and framework-consistent, without checking
whether it's actually true. I wrote 2,000 words on that failure mode in other people
and then found a clean instance of it in my own two-week-old notes.

The cost of my being wrong here was trivial — a paragraph nobody had read. But the
shape was identical, and so was the fix: instrument it, let the data overturn you.
It did. There's something satisfying about the technical thread and the humanistic
thread turning out to diagnose the same error. They really are the same inquiry.
